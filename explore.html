Annotation · Summary · the Evidence Room

In short: the story guides you through the cases in order; this room lets you test them yourself. It holds the same five datasets (Rome, Britain, Alexandria, London and the US dollar) with every figure traceable to its source row.

What the new spending data adds: nominal US spending rose 711-fold from 1913 to 2025, but 97 of every 100 of those 2025 dollars is the rise in prices. After inflation it still grew 22-fold, about 2.8% a year. That is the reason for hope beside the reason for concern; real spending fell in 31 of 112 years, most recently 2022-24.

What the evidence shows: every series loses value. At its lowest, Rome's denarius keeps about 3% of its Augustan silver, the last British staters about 27% of their gold, Ptolemaic bronze about 49% of its bronze per unit, and the Tudor penny about 17% of its 1526 silver. The dollar ends at about 3% of its 1913 purchasing power. Only London and Alexandria partly recover.

How to use it: start with Compare for the big picture, then use the coin exhibits to see single moments, and finish in the Ledger to check any number. The exhibits run from broad comparison to raw data:

  1. Compare the cases: all series indexed to 100 by years elapsed, or side by side in their own units and calendars; switch measures per case and hover for the source.
  2. The melting coins: one coin per record for any case, filled with what remains.
  3. Break a coin into 100 pieces: four 10 x 10 grids; pick any moment for each.
  4. Bronze at true weight: every Ptolemaic standard at relative size; click one to lay out all its denominations.
  5. The race to the bottom: all cases start on the same day; press play to run the years.
  6. What the dollars actually bought: nominal and real US spending on one axis, with log or linear scale, zoom presets and a brushable strip, plus a year-by-year inflation strip.
  7. Decade by decade: nominal against real growth for every decade, sortable, so the good decades and the bad ones separate.
  8. The dollar time machine: any amount between any two years, with the cents kept drawn out and real spending growth over the same span alongside.
  9. Rate of loss: sortable table of first, lowest, years to half and annual rate.
  10. The ledger: every row from the source files with method and confidence.
  • DataThe same figures as the story, from common.js; the original spreadsheets ship in the data folder.
